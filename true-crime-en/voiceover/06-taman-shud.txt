THE TAMAN SHUD CASE (1948, ADELAIDE, AUSTRALIA)
Read in order, with a pause of about one second between lines. Tone: calm, serious true-crime narrator.

Adelaide, Australia. December 1st, 1948. A man is found dead on Somerton Beach.

He is well dressed, but carries no identification. The labels have been cut from his clothes.

The autopsy suggests poison, but no poison is ever found.

Months later, investigators find a tiny rolled-up scrap of paper hidden in his trouser pocket.

It reads 'Tamám Shud', meaning 'ended' or 'finished'. The words were torn from a book of Persian poetry: the Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam.

A man later hands police the copy of the book it came from. It had been left in his car.

Inside the back cover: a phone number, and a mysterious code that has never been cracked.

For more than seventy years, no one knows who he is.

In 2022, researchers announced that DNA genealogy had identified him as Carl Webb, an electrical engineer from Melbourne.

But how he died, and why, remains a mystery.
