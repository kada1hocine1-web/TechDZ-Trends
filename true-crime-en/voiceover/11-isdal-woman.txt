THE ISDAL WOMAN (1970, BERGEN, NORWAY)
Read in order, with a pause of about one second between lines. Tone: calm, serious true-crime narrator.

Bergen, Norway. November 29th, 1970.

Hikers in a remote valley find the body of a woman, badly burned, surrounded by strange objects.

All the labels have been removed from her clothes and belongings.

Days later, police find two suitcases she left at Bergen railway station.

Inside: wigs, glasses without corrective lenses, and a notebook filled with coded entries.

Investigators discover she had traveled across Europe using at least nine different false identities.

Police officially conclude it was suicide. Many have never accepted that answer.

In 2017, isotope analysis of her teeth suggested she was born around 1930, near Nuremberg, Germany.

But her name, and her true story, remain unknown.

The Isdal Woman remains one of Norway's greatest mysteries.
