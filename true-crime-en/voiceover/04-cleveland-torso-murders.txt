THE CLEVELAND TORSO MURDERS (1934, OHIO, USA)
Read in order, with a pause of about one second between lines. Tone: calm, serious true-crime narrator.

Cleveland, Ohio. In the 1930s, the Great Depression fills the city's ravines with shantytowns.

In 1934, the remains of a woman wash up on the shore of Lake Erie. Locals call her the Lady of the Lake.

Over the next four years, more bodies are found, many around a ravine called Kingsbury Run.

Officially, there are at least twelve victims. All dismembered. Most never identified.

The city's Safety Director is a famous lawman: Eliot Ness, the man who took on Al Capone.

Ness launches a massive manhunt for the man the press calls the Mad Butcher of Kingsbury Run.

In 1938, he orders the shantytowns raided and burned. The killings appear to stop.

Suspects are arrested and questioned, but no one is ever convicted.

The Cleveland Torso Murders remain officially unsolved.
