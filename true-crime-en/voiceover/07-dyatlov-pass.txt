THE DYATLOV PASS INCIDENT (1959, URAL MOUNTAINS, USSR)
Read in order, with a pause of about one second between lines. Tone: calm, serious true-crime narrator.

The Ural Mountains, Soviet Union. January 1959.

Nine experienced hikers, led by 23-year-old Igor Dyatlov, set out on a winter trek.

On the night of February 1st, they make camp on the slope of a mountain called Kholat Syakhl.

Weeks later, searchers find the tent. It has been cut open from the inside.

Footprints show the group fled into the freezing night, many without shoes or proper clothing.

The bodies are found over the following months. Some died of hypothermia. Others had severe internal injuries.

Soviet investigators conclude only that they died because of a 'compelling natural force'.

Theories multiply: avalanches, secret military tests, even the supernatural.

In 2020, Russian prosecutors concluded that a slab avalanche had driven them from the tent.

But for many, the Dyatlov Pass incident is still not fully explained.
