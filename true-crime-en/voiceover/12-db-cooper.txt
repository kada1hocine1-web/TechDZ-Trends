D.B. COOPER (1971, WASHINGTON, USA)
Read in order, with a pause of about one second between lines. Tone: calm, serious true-crime narrator.

November 24th, 1971. A man boards a plane… and vanishes forever. This is the mystery of D.B. Cooper.

Dark suit, black clip-on tie. He buys a one-way ticket for twenty dollars on Northwest Orient Flight 305, from Portland to Seattle, under the name Dan Cooper.

Shortly after takeoff, he hands a note to a flight attendant. She ignores it. So he leans in and whispers: 'Miss, you'd better look at that note. I have a bomb.'

He opens his briefcase: red cylinders, wires.

His demands: two hundred thousand dollars in twenty-dollar bills, four parachutes, and a fuel truck waiting in Seattle.

The FBI delivers the ransom and the parachutes on the tarmac. Cooper releases the passengers… but keeps the crew.

He orders the plane to fly toward Mexico City, at ten thousand feet, landing gear down.

Somewhere over the dark, freezing, forested mountains of the Pacific Northwest, he lowers the rear stairs of the Boeing 727… and jumps into the night.

A massive manhunt follows. More than eight hundred suspects are questioned. Cooper is never found.

In 1980, a young boy digging on the bank of the Columbia River finds bundles of decaying bills: five thousand eight hundred dollars of the ransom.

But no trace of the hijacker, the rest of the money, or his parachute.

To this day, it remains the only unsolved case of air piracy in American aviation history.
