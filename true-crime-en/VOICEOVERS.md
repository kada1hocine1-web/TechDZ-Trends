# Voice-over scripts

One recording per video. Read the lines in order with a pause of about one second between them.

## 01. Jack The Ripper (1888, London, England)

London, 1888. In the crowded streets of Whitechapel, a killer strikes in the dark.

Between August and November, five women are murdered. Historians call them the canonical five.

Mary Ann Nichols. Annie Chapman. Elizabeth Stride. Catherine Eddowes. Mary Jane Kelly.

The killings are savage, and panic spreads across the city.

Police and newspapers receive hundreds of letters claiming to be from the killer.

One of them, the 'Dear Boss' letter, gives him the name the world will remember: Jack the Ripper.

Another, the 'From Hell' letter, is sent with half of a human kidney.

Scotland Yard investigates for months. Dozens of suspects are named, from doctors to drifters.

No one is ever charged.

More than a century later, the identity of Jack the Ripper remains one of history's greatest mysteries.

## 02. The Villisca Axe Murders (1912, Iowa, Usa)

Villisca, Iowa. June 1912. A quiet small town in the American Midwest.

On the night of June 9th, the Moore family returns home from a church program, with two young guests: the Stillinger sisters.

The next morning, the house is silent. Neighbors find all eight people inside murdered in their beds.

The weapon is an axe belonging to the father, Josiah Moore.

Strange details haunt investigators: every mirror and window had been covered with cloth.

Crowds trample through the house before the scene is secured, destroying evidence.

Several suspects emerge. A traveling preacher, Reverend George Kelly, is tried twice.

The first jury deadlocks. The second acquits him.

The case is never solved.

Today, the Villisca Axe Murder House still stands, and the killer's identity remains unknown.

## 03. The Hinterkaifeck Murders (1922, Bavaria, Germany)

Bavaria, Germany. 1922. An isolated farmstead called Hinterkaifeck.

Days before the end of March, farmer Andreas Gruber notices footprints in the snow, leading from the forest to the farm. None lead back.

He hears footsteps in the attic. A set of house keys goes missing. He mentions it to neighbors, but never goes to the police.

A new maid arrives on March 31st. The previous one had quit, saying the house was haunted.

That night, all six people on the farm are killed, including two children.

The bodies are discovered days later, on April 4th.

The most chilling detail: someone appears to have stayed on the farm after the murders, feeding the animals and eating food from the kitchen.

Police question over a hundred suspects over the decades. No one is ever charged.

The farmhouse is demolished the following year.

A century later, the Hinterkaifeck murders are still unsolved.

## 04. The Cleveland Torso Murders (1934, Ohio, Usa)

Cleveland, Ohio. In the 1930s, the Great Depression fills the city's ravines with shantytowns.

In 1934, the remains of a woman wash up on the shore of Lake Erie. Locals call her the Lady of the Lake.

Over the next four years, more bodies are found, many around a ravine called Kingsbury Run.

Officially, there are at least twelve victims. All dismembered. Most never identified.

The city's Safety Director is a famous lawman: Eliot Ness, the man who took on Al Capone.

Ness launches a massive manhunt for the man the press calls the Mad Butcher of Kingsbury Run.

In 1938, he orders the shantytowns raided and burned. The killings appear to stop.

Suspects are arrested and questioned, but no one is ever convicted.

The Cleveland Torso Murders remain officially unsolved.

## 05. The Black Dahlia (1947, California, Usa)

Los Angeles, January 15th, 1947.

In a vacant lot in the Leimert Park neighborhood, a woman walking with her child discovers a body.

The victim is Elizabeth Short, a 22-year-old aspiring actress.

The newspapers give her a name that will make her famous: the Black Dahlia.

The crime is brutal, and the press coverage is relentless.

Days later, a package arrives at the Los Angeles Examiner, containing her birth certificate, photos, and address book.

Dozens of people come forward to confess. None of the confessions hold up.

Detectives investigate hundreds of suspects.

But no one is ever charged.

More than 75 years later, the Black Dahlia murder remains one of Los Angeles's most famous unsolved cases.

## 06. The Taman Shud Case (1948, Adelaide, Australia)

Adelaide, Australia. December 1st, 1948. A man is found dead on Somerton Beach.

He is well dressed, but carries no identification. The labels have been cut from his clothes.

The autopsy suggests poison, but no poison is ever found.

Months later, investigators find a tiny rolled-up scrap of paper hidden in his trouser pocket.

It reads 'Tamám Shud', meaning 'ended' or 'finished'. The words were torn from a book of Persian poetry: the Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam.

A man later hands police the copy of the book it came from. It had been left in his car.

Inside the back cover: a phone number, and a mysterious code that has never been cracked.

For more than seventy years, no one knows who he is.

In 2022, researchers announced that DNA genealogy had identified him as Carl Webb, an electrical engineer from Melbourne.

But how he died, and why, remains a mystery.

## 07. The Dyatlov Pass Incident (1959, Ural Mountains, Ussr)

The Ural Mountains, Soviet Union. January 1959.

Nine experienced hikers, led by 23-year-old Igor Dyatlov, set out on a winter trek.

On the night of February 1st, they make camp on the slope of a mountain called Kholat Syakhl.

Weeks later, searchers find the tent. It has been cut open from the inside.

Footprints show the group fled into the freezing night, many without shoes or proper clothing.

The bodies are found over the following months. Some died of hypothermia. Others had severe internal injuries.

Soviet investigators conclude only that they died because of a 'compelling natural force'.

Theories multiply: avalanches, secret military tests, even the supernatural.

In 2020, Russian prosecutors concluded that a slab avalanche had driven them from the tent.

But for many, the Dyatlov Pass incident is still not fully explained.

## 08. The Beaumont Children (1966, Adelaide, Australia)

Adelaide, Australia. January 26th, 1966. Australia Day.

Three siblings, Jane, Arnna and Grant Beaumont, aged nine, seven and four, take the bus to Glenelg Beach.

They are expected home by midday. They never return.

Witnesses later report seeing the children playing with a tall, thin man with fair hair.

At a local shop, the children pay for their food with a one-pound note, more money than their mother had given them.

The disappearance triggers one of the largest searches in Australian history.

Over the decades, sites are excavated, and new leads emerge. None bring them home.

The Beaumont children have never been found.

Their disappearance changed Australia forever, and the case remains open to this day.

## 09. The Zodiac Killer (1968, California, Usa)

Northern California. December 1968.

A killer begins a series of attacks. Over the next ten months, five people are killed and two survive.

Then he starts writing to the newspapers.

He calls himself the Zodiac, and signs his letters with a crosshair symbol.

He sends coded messages, ciphers, and demands they be printed on front pages.

One cipher is cracked within days by a schoolteacher and his wife.

Another, the 340 cipher, resists for 51 years. Amateur codebreakers finally solve it in 2020.

In his letters, the Zodiac claims as many as 37 victims.

Thousands of suspects are investigated. No one is ever charged.

The Zodiac Killer was never caught.

## 10. The Monster Of Florence (1968, Florence, Italy)

Florence, Italy. Between 1968 and 1985, the hills around the city become a hunting ground.

Eight couples are murdered, sixteen victims in all, attacked at night in parked cars in secluded spots.

Ballistics link the killings to the same .22 caliber Beretta pistol.

The press names the killer 'Il Mostro di Firenze', the Monster of Florence.

The investigation becomes one of the longest and most controversial in Italian history.

In 1994, a farmer named Pietro Pacciani is convicted. His conviction is overturned on appeal. He dies in 1998 before a retrial.

Two of his acquaintances are later convicted for some of the murders.

But many investigators and journalists doubt that the whole truth was ever uncovered.

The gun was never found.

Decades later, the true identity of the Monster of Florence is still debated.

## 11. The Isdal Woman (1970, Bergen, Norway)

Bergen, Norway. November 29th, 1970.

Hikers in a remote valley find the body of a woman, badly burned, surrounded by strange objects.

All the labels have been removed from her clothes and belongings.

Days later, police find two suitcases she left at Bergen railway station.

Inside: wigs, glasses without corrective lenses, and a notebook filled with coded entries.

Investigators discover she had traveled across Europe using at least nine different false identities.

Police officially conclude it was suicide. Many have never accepted that answer.

In 2017, isotope analysis of her teeth suggested she was born around 1930, near Nuremberg, Germany.

But her name, and her true story, remain unknown.

The Isdal Woman remains one of Norway's greatest mysteries.

## 12. D.B. Cooper (1971, Washington, Usa)

November 24th, 1971. A man boards a plane… and vanishes forever. This is the mystery of D.B. Cooper.

Dark suit, black clip-on tie. He buys a one-way ticket for twenty dollars on Northwest Orient Flight 305, from Portland to Seattle, under the name Dan Cooper.

Shortly after takeoff, he hands a note to a flight attendant. She ignores it. So he leans in and whispers: 'Miss, you'd better look at that note. I have a bomb.'

He opens his briefcase: red cylinders, wires.

His demands: two hundred thousand dollars in twenty-dollar bills, four parachutes, and a fuel truck waiting in Seattle.

The FBI delivers the ransom and the parachutes on the tarmac. Cooper releases the passengers… but keeps the crew.

He orders the plane to fly toward Mexico City, at ten thousand feet, landing gear down.

Somewhere over the dark, freezing, forested mountains of the Pacific Northwest, he lowers the rear stairs of the Boeing 727… and jumps into the night.

A massive manhunt follows. More than eight hundred suspects are questioned. Cooper is never found.

In 1980, a young boy digging on the bank of the Columbia River finds bundles of decaying bills: five thousand eight hundred dollars of the ransom.

But no trace of the hijacker, the rest of the money, or his parachute.

To this day, it remains the only unsolved case of air piracy in American aviation history.

## 13. The Alphabet Murders (1971, New York, Usa)

Rochester, New York. Between 1971 and 1973.

Three young girls, aged ten and eleven, are abducted and murdered.

Their names: Carmen Colón. Wanda Walkowicz. Michelle Maenza.

Each girl's first and last names begin with the same letter.

And each was found in a town starting with that same letter: Churchville, Webster, and Macedon.

The press calls them the Alphabet Murders, or the Double Initial Murders.

Hundreds of people are questioned. Several suspects are investigated and later ruled out.

No one has ever been charged.

Half a century later, the families are still waiting for answers.

## 14. The Oakland County Child Killer (1976, Michigan, Usa)

Oakland County, Michigan. 1976.

Over thirteen months, four children are abducted and killed.

Mark Stebbins. Jill Robinson. Kristine Mihelich. Timothy King.

Their bodies are found days later, left in public places, carefully laid out.

Fear grips the suburbs of Detroit. Parents stop letting their children go out alone.

A task force launches what was then one of the largest murder investigations in American history.

Thousands of tips are examined. Over the years, DNA testing rules out several suspects.

No one is ever charged.

The case remains open, and investigators still hope for a breakthrough.

## 15. The Burger Chef Murders (1978, Indiana, Usa)

Speedway, Indiana. November 17th, 1978.

Four young employees close up a Burger Chef restaurant for the night.

Jayne Friedt, Ruth Ellen Shelton, Daniel Davis and Mark Flemmonds. The oldest is twenty.

Later that night, the restaurant is found unlocked and empty. The day's earnings are gone.

At first, police think the staff took the money and left. The scene is cleaned before it is properly examined.

Two days later, the four are found dead in a wooded area in a neighboring county.

Investigators follow thousands of leads over the decades.

No one has ever been charged.

The Burger Chef murders remain one of Indiana's most haunting unsolved cases.

## 16. The Chicago Tylenol Murders (1982, Illinois, Usa)

Chicago, Illinois. September 1982.

A twelve-year-old girl takes a capsule of Extra-Strength Tylenol for a cold. She dies hours later.

Within days, six more people in the Chicago area die the same way.

The capsules have been laced with potassium cyanide.

Investigators believe someone took bottles from stores, poisoned them, and put them back on the shelves.

Johnson & Johnson recalls around 31 million bottles across the United States.

A man who sent an extortion letter demanding one million dollars is convicted of extortion, but never charged with the murders.

The tragedy leads to tamper-resistant packaging, still used on medicines today.

But the poisoner was never caught.

## 17. Jonbenét Ramsey (1996, Colorado, Usa)

Boulder, Colorado. December 26th, 1996.

Early in the morning, Patsy Ramsey calls the police. Her six-year-old daughter, JonBenét, is missing.

On the stairs, she has found a long, handwritten ransom note demanding 118,000 dollars.

That afternoon, JonBenét is found dead in the basement of the family home.

The investigation is criticized from the start. The house was not fully secured, and evidence may have been compromised.

For years, suspicion and media attention fall on the family.

In 2006, a man falsely confessed to the murder. DNA ruled him out.

In 2008, new DNA testing leads the district attorney to formally clear the Ramsey family, and apologize.

Boulder police say the investigation is still active.

Nearly three decades later, JonBenét's killer has never been identified.

## 18. The Notorious B.I.G. (1997, California, Usa)

Los Angeles. March 9th, 1997.

Christopher Wallace, the Notorious B.I.G., is one of the biggest stars in hip-hop.

That night, he leaves a music industry party at the Petersen Automotive Museum.

His SUV stops at a red light. A dark car pulls up alongside.

A gunman opens fire. Biggie is rushed to the hospital, but dies. He was 24 years old.

His death comes six months after the killing of Tupac Shakur in Las Vegas.

Theories point to the bitter East Coast–West Coast rivalry, and even to allegations involving police officers.

Investigations, lawsuits, books and documentaries follow.

But no one has ever been charged with his murder.

His second album, released just over two weeks later, was titled Life After Death.

## 19. The Setagaya Family Murder (2000, Tokyo, Japan)

Setagaya, Tokyo. The night of December 30th, 2000.

The Miyazawa family, a father, a mother, and their two young children, are at home.

An intruder enters through a bathroom window and kills all four.

Then, instead of fleeing, the killer stays in the house for hours.

He uses the family computer, eats food from the fridge, and even treats his own wounds.

He leaves behind fingerprints, his blood, and clothes. Evidence that should solve any case.

DNA suggests that his mother's family line may trace back to southern Europe.

Yet his fingerprints and DNA match no one in Japan's databases.

More than two decades later, Tokyo police are still asking the public for help.

The killer has never been identified.

## 20. Madeleine Mccann (2007, Praia Da Luz, Portugal)

Praia da Luz, Portugal. May 3rd, 2007.

A British family is on holiday at a resort on the Algarve coast.

Three-year-old Madeleine McCann and her younger twin siblings sleep in the apartment, while their parents dine with friends at a restaurant nearby.

The parents take turns checking on the children.

At around 10 p.m., Madeleine's mother discovers she is gone.

The disappearance becomes one of the most heavily reported missing person cases in modern history.

Police in Portugal, Britain and Germany have investigated for years. Scotland Yard opened its own investigation in 2011.

In 2020, German prosecutors named a suspect, who has not been charged in her case.

Madeleine has never been found.

Her family continues to search, and the case remains open.
